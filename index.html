Checks

Six tests. No blended score.

These are the checks a data team already runs by hand in the first weeks of a trial. The public benchmark is reported revenue, using the SEC filing date. There is no price panel and no Sharpe ratio.

File adequacy

Event date, first-seen date, and a revision flag. A zero is not a missing value unless the manifest says so.

Entity match

Weighted coverage of the buyer's names. A fuzzy guess can propose a FIGI. It cannot write one.

Coverage shape

Whether the panel stays stable through time, including names that later delisted.

Point in time

Two drops of the same week agree, or the change carries a new first-seen time.

Known events

Closures, outages, or layoffs move the series the way the public record says they should. The event list is frozen before the run.

Public benchmark

Rank correlation with reported revenue, counted only once the filing date says the data was knowable.

Verdicts

First matching rule wins.

Renegotiate is the useful outcome in a live trial. It says what to fix, what to cut from the price, and which history must not be backtested.

Kill

The weighted match is too thin, or known events fail in the wrong direction. Stop the trial and send the misses back.

Renegotiate

A relationship may exist, but the history was rebuilt, the panel jumped, or the fit shrinks once publication lag is applied.

Continue

Mapping holds on small names, revisions are logged, and a pre-registered public test still points the right way after lag.
